From Darwin to Deep Convolutional Networks

In 1872, Charles Darwin published The Expression of the Emotions in Man and Animals, postulating that human facial expressions are universal, biologically rooted, and evolved as communicative survival mechanisms. A century later, psychologists Dr. Paul Ekman and Wallace Friesen codified this hypothesis into the Facial Action Coding System (FACS).

FACS breaks down human facial behavior into 46 anatomically independent Action Units (AUs). Every smile, grimace, gasp, or frown is an orchestration of these minute muscle contractions. In modern computer vision, deep learning models learn high-dimensional spatial filters that map directly to these Action Units without requiring manual feature engineering.

Macro-Expressions

Last between 0.5 to 4 seconds, visible across normal interactions, often voluntarily modulated by social display rules.

Micro-Expressions

Flash involuntarily in 1/25th to 1/5th of a second (< 500ms), revealing repressed or subconscious affective states.

Russell's Circumplex Model

Emotions are mapped along two orthogonal dimensions: Valence (pleasure vs displeasure) and Arousal (activation vs deactivation).

Anatomy of the 7 Primary Emotions

Discover the exact muscle contractions, FACS Action Units, and visual markers that our neural network inspects.

😊 Happiness / Joy Valence: +0.81 | Arousal: +0.51

1. Happiness (The Duchenne Marker)

Positive Valence • High Harmony

True happiness is biologically distinguished by the Duchenne smile. Unlike polite or social smiles which only engage the mouth, genuine amusement triggers the involuntary outer bands of the eye sockets.

Key Action Units:
  • AU6 (Cheek Raiser): Orbicularis oculi muscle pulls cheeks upwards, creating crow's feet wrinkles.
  • AU12 (Lip Corner Puller): Zygomaticus major muscle pulls lip corners obliquely up toward the temples.
CNN Feature Keys:

High gradient variance around nasolabial folds, upward parabolic curvature of mouth boundary, and eyelid squinting.

😢 Sadness Valence: -0.63 | Arousal: -0.27

2. Sadness & Grief

Negative Valence • Low Arousal

Sadness is one of the hardest expressions to feign convincingly because contracting the inner corners of the eyebrows independently (AU1) without the outer corners is biologically rare under conscious control.

Key Action Units:
  • AU1 (Inner Brow Raiser): Frontalis pars medialis muscle draws inner brows upward into a triangle.
  • AU4 (Brow Lowerer): Corrugator supercilii knits brows slightly inward.
  • AU15 (Lip Corner Depressor): Depressor anguli oris pulls mouth corners downward.
CNN Feature Keys:

Downward mouth arc, lowered eyelid lids, horizontal crease across the forehead center.

😲 Surprise Valence: +0.40 | Arousal: +0.67

3. Surprise & Astonishment

Neutral/Positive • High Arousal

Surprise is the briefest of all emotional states. Its evolutionary utility is to quickly maximize sensory intake by widening the visual field (dilating pupils, raising brows) and preparing the lungs for sudden flight or fight.

Key Action Units:
  • AU1 + AU2 (Inner & Outer Brow Raiser): Arches brows high across the forehead.
  • AU5 (Upper Lid Raiser): Widens palpebral fissure, exposing sclera above iris.
  • AU26/27 (Jaw Drop / Mouth Stretch): Pterygoid relaxation drops the mandible.
CNN Feature Keys:

High circular aspect ratio in mouth region, expanded white sclera around pupils, raised eyebrow distance from eye center.

😠 Anger Valence: -0.51 | Arousal: +0.59

4. Anger & Rage

Negative Valence • High Arousal

Anger signals an offensive or confrontational stance. Blood rushes to the hands and face, facial features contract into a menacing, focused tunnel vision aimed at the adversary.

Key Action Units:
  • AU4 (Brow Lowerer): Corrugator supercilii pulls eyebrows downward and together into a steep V-shape.
  • AU7 (Lid Tightener): Narrowed lower eyelids create a penetrating gaze.
  • AU23/24 (Lip Tightener / Pressor): Orbicularis oris compresses the lips into a firm red margin.
CNN Feature Keys:

Vertical glabella furrows between the eyes, reduced mouth thickness, sharp downward eyebrow slant.

😨 Fear Valence: -0.64 | Arousal: +0.60

5. Fear & Terror

High Threat Response

Fear primes the cardiovascular system for defensive reaction. Unlike surprise, the eyebrows in fear are pulled both up and horizontally together into a flattened tension, with lips stretched sideways.

Key Action Units:
  • AU1 + AU2 + AU4: Brow is elevated and furrowed simultaneously.
  • AU5 (Upper Lid Raiser): Wide eyes expose maximum perimeter sclera.
  • AU20 (Lip Stretcher): Risorius muscle retracts the mouth horizontally toward the ears.
CNN Feature Keys:

Stretched horizontal mouth perimeter, widened eye sclera, forehead tension creases.

🤢 Disgust Valence: -0.60 | Arousal: +0.35

6. Disgust & Revulsion

Aversive Sensory Rejection

Evolved to protect against toxins, spoiled pathogens, and noxious odors. The physiological response closes off the nasal cavity and expels matter from the oral chamber.

Key Action Units:
  • AU9 (Nose Wrinkler): Levator labii superioris alaeque nasi draws the bridge of the nose into horizontal skin folds.
  • AU10 (Upper Lip Raiser): Elevates the central upper lip, exposing upper teeth.
CNN Feature Keys:

High edge gradient frequency across the nasal bridge, asymmetric upper lip elevation, narrowed eye apertures.

😐 Neutral Valence: 0.00 | Arousal: 0.00

7. Neutral Baseline

Homeostatic Equilibrium

The absence of active emotional valence or arousal. Serves as the critical baseline against which differential motion vectors and facial landmark displacements are quantified.

Key Action Units:

No active Action Units. Facial tonicity remains in relaxed bilateral symmetry with unpuckered lips and level eye gaze.

CNN Feature Keys:

Balanced horizontal landmark distances, lack of localized high-frequency edge clusters around mouth or forehead.
